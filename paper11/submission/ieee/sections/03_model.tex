\section{Operating Model}
\label{sec:model}

We model a public-sector security operations team that, each maintenance window, can
remediate at most $K$ \mbox{(host, CVE)} pairs drawn from a backlog of applicable
vulnerabilities across an $\sim$830-host fleet ($\sim$3{,}500 pairs per seed). The team
wants to spend that budget on the pairs that most reduce \emph{mission risk}: high
exploit-likelihood CVEs on high-context hosts. $K=50$ models a tight triage window;
$K=250$ models a well-resourced window. The swept axis is capacity $K$, not host count.

No employer or operational data is used at any stage. All fleets are synthetic with
public structural priors (Section~\ref{sec:method}), and each CVE's EPSS score and KEV
membership are resampled from the real corpus, a 2026-06-05 snapshot of the FIRST EPSS
feed intersected with the CISA KEV catalog, filtered to 203{,}174 CVEs disclosed in 2020
or later. This isolates the contribution of asset context: the exploit signal is real and
identical in distribution across all compared methods, so any ranking difference is due to
how each method uses host context, not to differing exploit data.
